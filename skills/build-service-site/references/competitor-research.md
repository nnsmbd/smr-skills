## Research frame

Define before browsing:

- audience, geography, language, offer category, and price context;
- direct competitors, adjacent alternatives, and aspirational references;
- the decisions research must inform;
- the review date because sites and offers change.

Do not call a page reviewed until it was opened or supplied as a screenshot. If access is blocked, record the limitation and ask for materials only when they materially unblock the work.

## Competitor matrix

For each reviewed site capture:

1. Five-second impression.
2. Audience and problem clarity.
3. Hero offer, proof, and CTA.
4. Page narrative and section order.
5. Cases, testimonials, logos, founder presence, metrics, and legal trust.
6. Objection handling, FAQ, guarantee, pricing, and form friction.
7. User path from landing to contact or purchase.
8. Mobile hierarchy and interaction.
9. Voice, jargon, specificity, and unsupported claims.
10. Transferable principles, anti-patterns, and open hypotheses.

Separate a competitor's visible behavior from assumptions about its conversion performance. Popularity, awards, visual polish, and templates are not conversion proof.

## Reference-source roles

Use sources by property:

- real product libraries: flows, navigation, form patterns, and interaction states;
- award galleries: art direction, composition, typography, and expressive motion;
- design-system libraries: colors, typography, spacing, surfaces, and component language;
- template marketplaces: full-page rhythm, responsive arrangements, and implementation patterns;
- direct competitors: offer, proof, objections, language, and market expectations.

For every saved reference record:

- URL, date, screenshot or captured evidence;
- what specific property is relevant;
- what may transfer;
- what must not be copied;
- accessibility, performance, licensing, and responsive risks.

Do not combine whole sites. A design direction may combine a small set of compatible principles while remaining original to the current brand and content.

## Evidence labels

Label findings consistently:

- `fact`: directly visible or supplied;
- `observation`: pattern across visible facts;
- `inference`: plausible conclusion requiring validation;
- `inspiration`: visual or interaction idea, not evidence;
- `hypothesis`: measurable change to test after a baseline exists.

Do not convert percentages or claims from third-party analyses into facts without their original evidence.

## Deliverables

Produce:

- a compact competitor table;
- a reference board grouped by transferable property;
- messaging gaps and trust gaps;
- design opportunities and risks;
- a shortlist for three visual directions;
- a post-launch experiment backlog kept separate from launch requirements.
