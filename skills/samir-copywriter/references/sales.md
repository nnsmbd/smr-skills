# Sales Mode

Write sales copy to help a specific reader take a specific next action for an evidence-backed reason. Do not confuse persuasion with hype, pressure, or decorative formulas.

## Build the commercial brief

Establish:

1. `Product`: what is sold, delivered, and excluded.
2. `Audience`: role, situation, existing alternatives, language, and buying context.
3. `Problem or desire`: what matters now in the audience's words.
4. `Awareness`: whether readers know the problem, available solutions, this product, or the offer.
5. `Offer`: deliverables, price or commercial model when available, terms, bonuses, guarantee, and next step.
6. `Mechanism`: why this solution should work and how it differs.
7. `Proof`: cases, demonstrations, data, process evidence, credentials, reviews, and limitations.
8. `Objections`: risk, effort, trust, fit, timing, cost, and switching friction.
9. `Context`: traffic source, funnel position, page or asset type, and message match.
10. `Action`: the single primary behavior the copy should earn.

Do not invent missing commercial facts. Ask when a missing fact changes the promise or offer; otherwise use a visible placeholder.

Do not turn missing proof into a prominent section of publishable copy by default. Surface the gap before the draft, then use honest process evidence, a sample deliverable, methodology, clear scope, or a low-friction next step when available. State the absence publicly only when the user asks, the market requires disclosure, or the limitation itself materially builds trust.

## Research

When research is in scope:

- prioritize real customer language from interviews, calls, reviews, support messages, surveys, forums, search queries, and approved analytics;
- record the exact source and context of every voice-of-customer phrase;
- separate observed evidence, user-provided facts, inference, and inspiration;
- compare competitors by offer, mechanism, proof, objections, CTA, and positioning rather than copying their sentences;
- verify current, unstable, or high-stakes facts with primary sources.

Create a compact voice-of-customer table when evidence is substantial:

| Phrase or pattern | Source | Situation | Possible use |
|---|---|---|---|

Do not make a phrase sound common merely because one person said it.

## Keep a claims ledger

For any material claim, track:

| Claim | Status | Evidence | Allowed use |
|---|---|---|---|
| exact user-provided fact | `provided` | brief or file | use with attribution or context |
| independently confirmed fact | `verified` | opened primary source | use within source limits |
| missing proof or wording | `placeholder` | none yet | keep visibly marked; do not publish |
| deceptive, contradicted, or unusable claim | `rejected` | conflict or policy risk | exclude |

Claims include metrics, timeframes, guarantees, research, rankings, client names, testimonials, affiliations, scarcity, and urgency.

Never fabricate a precise number to make copy feel credible. Never imply an endorsement or relationship that does not exist. Use urgency only when the reason and deadline are real.

## Choose message architecture

Select structure from the reader, offer, evidence, and action. Use AIDA, PAS, BAB, awareness levels, objection handling, and direct-response patterns as optional lenses, not compulsory templates.

Common architectures:

### Short B2B service page

`specific promise → who it is for → mechanism → proof → process → risk reduction → CTA`

### Expert or system offer

`business diagnosis → desired operating state → method → deliverables → implementation support → proof → fit → offer → CTA`

### Long-form direct response

`hook → problem or desire → new explanation → mechanism → proof → solution → objections → offer → risk reduction → CTA`

Use only when the offer and traffic justify the length. Do not adopt aggression or profanity by default.

### Reputation or legacy page

`purpose → history → people → scale → outcomes → gratitude or meaning → next step`

This may strengthen trust without behaving like a classic sales page.

## Draft by sections

For a long page, draft and verify the message hierarchy before polishing every sentence. Keep one purpose per section. Maintain message match between the source ad, page, form, and next step.

Translate features through consequences:

`feature → functional result → business or life effect → evidence`

Stop before inventing an emotional payoff the product cannot support.

## Proof and objections

- Prefer demonstrations and specific case context over adjectives.
- When outcome proof is unavailable, strengthen trust with verifiable process proof: what is inspected, what the buyer receives, how decisions are made, who performs the work, and what is explicitly outside scope.
- Present a testimonial as supplied; do not strengthen the result or make it typical without evidence.
- Match each proof element to a real objection.
- State limitations when they help the right buyer self-select.
- Use guarantees only with exact approved terms.
- Distinguish who is a fit and who is not without manufactured exclusivity.

## Output

Return one coherent draft by default. For high-leverage testing, add a small set of meaningfully different headline, hook, offer-angle, or CTA variants. Label the variable being tested; do not present random rephrasings as a test plan.

Do not append an explanation of persuasion principles unless the user asks. If strategic uncertainty remains, surface it before the publishable copy.
