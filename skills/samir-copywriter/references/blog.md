# Blog Mode

Write blog content as an honest continuation of Samir's work and thinking. Prefer a real observation with reader value over a manufactured lesson or funnel.

## Brief

Establish:

- what happened, changed, or was learned;
- why Samir cares now;
- which concrete details can make it real;
- what the reader should understand, try, feel, or discuss;
- the channel and approximate length;
- whether the goal is conversation, documentation, authority, traffic, or announcement.

If the prompt contains only a topic, do not invent a personal story. Either write a non-personal explainer or ask for the missing lived detail when personal experience is essential.

## Choose a subtype

### Build in public

Use for launches, prototypes, experiments, and work in progress.

1. Lead with the result or announcement.
2. Add one human detail about how it happened.
3. State the current limitation early.
4. Explain what exists now.
5. Show what comes next.
6. Ask for bugs, ideas, or feedback without begging for engagement.

### Reflection or discovery

Use for personal observations, identity, work, discipline, or philosophy.

1. Lead with the realization.
2. Ground it in the situation that produced it.
3. Turn it into a small framework or next action.
4. Keep early thinking provisional; do not present one experience as a universal law.
5. End with a real question only when the author wants conversation.

### Practical explainer

Use for tools, methods, routines, and systems.

1. Name the tool and its practical promise.
2. Translate it into plain language.
3. Explain how Samir actually uses it.
4. Organize the setup into numbered sections.
5. Include constraints and what may change later.
6. Give a useful next step without forcing a sale.

### Channel roadmap

Use for new themes or editorial direction.

1. State what is changing and what remains the same.
2. Group the new directions.
3. For each direction, connect personal interest to expected reader value.
4. Avoid promising a publishing cadence that is not decided.

### Weekly log

Use for work and life updates.

1. Open with the period or central mood.
2. Separate work and life only when it improves scanning.
3. Keep each point concrete enough to mean something.
4. Finish with the next action or invite readers to share their week.

### Digest

Treat the digest as a distinct editorial product.

1. Open with issue number, date, and the volume of material reviewed when verified.
2. Rank the main topics instead of listing everything equally.
3. Attribute claims and participant statements accurately.
4. Link to source threads when available.
5. Separate reported discussion from Samir's own conclusion.
6. End with a compact `От себя` synthesis.

Never fabricate a quote, participant, message count, usage number, link, or consensus.

## Hooks

Prefer one of these grounded openings:

- the thing shipped or changed;
- the honest problem;
- the surprising detail;
- the realization;
- a blunt translation of an overcomplicated concept;
- a concrete number with context.

Avoid repeating the title as the first sentence. Avoid `В современном мире`, `Сегодня я хочу поговорить`, `Давайте разберёмся`, and generic motivational proclamations.

## Endings

Choose the smallest ending that fits:

- a feedback request;
- a specific question;
- the next experiment;
- a useful link;
- one compact concluding thought.

Do not add `подпишись`, an engagement question, or a moral to every post. Preserve posts that are simply observations.

## Blog-sales boundary

A blog post may support reputation or demand, but do not turn it into sales copy unless the user asks. If a commercial CTA is needed, keep the personal observation intact and make the transition explicit rather than hiding a pitch inside artificial vulnerability.
