# Prose Layers

Describe style through observable evidence rather than broad personality adjectives. Analyze five independent layers.

## 1. Lexicon

Record preferred, permitted, avoided, and rejected words or constructions. Include the context and an alternative when useful. Separate legitimate technical vocabulary from technical language added for performance.

## 2. Syntax

Observe sentence construction: length mix, clause depth, fragments, questions, parentheses, list grammar, active/passive choices, and forms of address. Do not copy accidental grammar errors as style.

## 3. Rhythm

Observe how sentences and paragraphs interact: pauses, short punches, explanation length, whitespace, repetition, and where humor appears. Do not impose fixed quotas. Infer channel-specific tendencies from approved examples.

## 4. Structure

Observe openings, section order, transitions, headings, proof placement, and endings. Judge the scenario against the asset's central promise. A structurally irrelevant fact remains irrelevant even when accurate or amusing.

## 5. Voice markers

Record distinctive recurring moves such as a serious claim followed by a blunt translation, a self-deprecating final item in a list, an honest limitation, or a restrained aside. Ration markers; repetition turns recognition into self-parody.

## Core versus channel overrides

Store only stable cross-channel evidence in the core profile. Put channel-specific behavior in the channel profile. When evidence conflicts, preserve the split instead of averaging it into a generic rule.

## Evidence classes

Rank evidence in this order:

1. direct user approval or rejection;
2. user's final edited copy;
3. repeated pattern across independent approved sources;
4. one approved source;
5. model inference or platform convention.

Do not convert class 5 into a claim about Samir's voice.
