## Route and brief

Use this track for Instagram posts and carousel slides, Reels covers, Stories, Telegram visuals, website or article imagery, editorial posters, announcements, campaign visuals, and experimental art-directed images for Samir's personal brand.

Use this pipeline:

```text
request -> context -> communication goal -> visual concept -> art direction
-> CreativeSpec -> generation -> visual QA -> iteration/adaptation
```

Extract only relevant inputs:

- purpose and destination;
- content topic;
- core idea, tension, or emotion;
- desired viewer reaction;
- visual concept, when already supplied;
- format and aspect ratio;
- desired amount of visible text;
- realism versus abstraction;
- art direction;
- references and their roles.

Do not require product, offer, pain, proof, CTA, or a performance framework unless the request actually uses them.

## Brand-state model

Classify every brand instruction as one of three states:

### Fixed brand rules

Use only rules explicitly supplied or approved as durable: logos, palette, typography, recurring layout rules, or other established identity elements. These may receive a `BRAND LOCK`.

### Current visual direction

Treat this as a temporary system for one image, series, or campaign. Preserve it within the approved scope, but do not promote it into permanent brand identity.

### Experimental direction

Treat this as intentional exploration of a new visual language. Permit broader variation while preserving the communication idea and any explicit subject or copy locks.

If no visual system exists, offer 2-4 directions, explain their meaningful differences, recommend one, and use the selected direction only for the current scope.

## Reference use

Assign references roles such as:

- style;
- composition;
- mood;
- typography;
- photography;
- texture/material;
- general art direction.

Extract transferable principles instead of copying a finished identity:

- hierarchy and focal point;
- contrast and scale;
- spacing and density;
- lighting and depth;
- texture and material behavior;
- visual tension;
- type treatment;
- composition;
- level of abstraction.

State what may transfer and what must not transfer. Do not carry over another creator's logo, text, product identity, face, proprietary character, or complete composition.

## Concept development

When the user asks for ideas, use:

```text
Name:
Core visual idea:
Metaphor or central object:
Composition:
Art direction:
Mood:
Typography treatment:
Why it fits the content:
```

Omit fields that do not apply. Concepts must differ in idea and visual language, not just palette.

Tie aesthetic decisions to the communication goal. A striking image that obscures the core idea is not a successful personal-brand visual.

## Art-direction range

Personal-brand work may explore:

- editorial photography;
- conceptual or surreal scenes;
- cinematic imagery;
- graphic poster systems;
- experimental typography;
- collage and mixed media;
- 3D and material studies;
- abstract visual metaphors;
- deliberately raw or documentary photography.

Choose the range because it strengthens the content idea. Avoid generic AI spectacle, unexplained decoration, default neon gradients, arbitrary floating objects, and cinematic effects without narrative purpose.

## Locks and continuity

Use locks selectively:

- `SUBJECT LOCK`: preserve a person, object, or character.
- `COPY LOCK`: preserve exact visible strings.
- `COMPOSITION LOCK`: preserve an approved arrangement and hierarchy.
- `STYLE LOCK`: preserve the chosen visual language within one series.
- `BRAND LOCK`: use only for actual fixed brand rules.

Never enable `BRAND LOCK` automatically. When adapting a series, preserve the approved concept and relevant locks while recomposing for each destination.

Evaluate the final visual for idea fidelity, focal point, hierarchy, intentional art direction, mood, exact copy, artifacts, locks, and destination fit. Do not judge personal-brand work only by direct-response advertising criteria.
